This module has no theory to learn and nothing to remember for an exam. Its job is to get you to the starting line: you know how the lessons work, you have a place to type commands, your editor is installed, and you have checked all of it. When you finish it, Module 1 begins with "open your terminal", and you can.

## What you will learn

- How every lesson is built, what the `mylab` project is, and where your progress is saved.
- What kind of computer the course needs, and what to do when yours is old, slow or not yours.
- The three ways to practice: the practice terminal in the page, GitHub Codespaces, and Linux on your own computer.
- How to install Ubuntu on Windows with WSL, or prepare the terminal on macOS and Linux.
- How to install Visual Studio Code and connect it to your Linux.
- How to use an AI assistant as a tutor without letting it do the learning for you.
- How to prove that your setup is ready.

## Before you start

You need nothing. No earlier module, no knowledge of the terminal, no programming.

You need a computer with a real keyboard and an internet connection. Lesson 0.2 tells you if your computer is good enough, and lesson 0.3 gives you two ways to follow the course when it is not.

## Time

7 lessons, about 2 hours of reading and practice. Add the time your computer needs for downloads and one restart, which depends on your connection.

## Project step

The project of this course is a folder called `mylab`, and it does not exist yet: you create it at the end of [Module 1](../linux/). The project step of this module prepares the ground. You go through a checklist of eight lines, and you write and run a small script that tells you which tools are present on your system and which are missing. When every line is ticked, you are ready.
